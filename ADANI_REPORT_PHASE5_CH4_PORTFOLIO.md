## 4.2 Ports and SEZ Business

### Business Model

Adani Ports operates India's largest private port network, managing multiple terminals across different port facilities. The business operates on concession models where government grants operational contracts with contractually defined revenue streams.

**Core Economics:**
- **Revenue Drivers:** Cargo volume × tariff per unit + ancillary services
- **Cost Structure:** Fixed costs (labor, depreciation) + variable costs (energy, repairs)
- **Capital Intensity:** High upfront (~$1-2 billion per new port), then depreciation-based
- **Cash Generation:** Strong after capex decline (40%+ EBITDA margins, 20%+ FCF)

### Operational Scale (2025)

- **Total Capacity:** 280+ MTPA (Million Tonnes Per Annum)
- **Major Facilities:** Mundra, Hazira, Dhamra, regional contracts (Visakhapatnam, Paradip)
- **Cargo Types:** Coal (40%), containerized (20%), bulk liquids (20%), others (20%)
- **Market Position:** ~20% of India's private port cargo

### Financial Characteristics

- **Revenue Scale:** $6-8 billion annually
- **EBITDA Margin:** 40-50% (among highest in infrastructure)
- **Capex Requirements:** $500-800 million annually for capacity expansion
- **Return Profile:** High (20%+ IRR on expansion projects)

### Strategic Position

**Competitive Advantages:**
- Scale and diversified cargo mix
- Geographic positioning (western coast, high traffic routes)
- Government relationships and concession security
- Integrated commodity trading reducing terminal utilization risk

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Commodity price cycles affecting shipping patterns
- Regulatory pricing controls limiting upside
- Port congestion creating service delays
- Competition from other port operators

### Growth Trajectory

- Current capacity utilization: ~75%
- Expansion pipeline: 100+ MTPA in development
- CAGR through 2035: 5-7% (slightly above historical 4-5%)
- Capital deployment: $5-8 billion through 2035

## 4.3 Renewable Energy Business

### Business Model and Strategic Importance

Adani Green Energy represents the group's long-term strategic positioning toward energy transition. The business develops, owns, and operates renewable power generation assets primarily on long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs).

**Business Model:**
- **Revenue:** PPA contracts with government and large industrial users
- **Economics:** Capital-intensive upfront, then strong FCF generation
- **Scalability:** Modular asset development allowing phased expansion
- **ESG Appeal:** Institutional investor preference driving valuations

### Operational Scale (2025)

- **Renewable Capacity:** 11+ GW (one of world's largest)
- **Composition:** Solar (~7 GW), Wind (~3 GW), Hybrid/Storage (emerging)
- **PPA Coverage:** ~90%+ of capacity under long-term contracts
- **Technology:** Mix of crystalline solar, thin-film, and wind turbines

### Financial Characteristics

- **Revenue Scale:** $1.5-2 billion annually
- **EBITDA Margin:** 75-80% (among highest for renewables)
- **Capex Requirements:** $2-3 billion annually for capacity expansion
- **Cost of Generation:** $2-3/MWh (competitive globally)

### Strategic Importance

**Portfolio Rationale:**
- Hedges thermal power decline through renewable growth
- Meets ESG investor demand and institution investor preferences
- Provides long-term visibility through PPA contracts
- Captures structural energy transition tailwind

**Capital Market Reception:**
- Highest valuation multiple within group
- Institutional investor darling (ESG focus)
- De facto "clean energy" platform for group
- Supports overall group ESG narrative

### Growth Trajectory

- Target: 45-50 GW by 2030 (4x from 2025)
- Capex requirement: $15-20 billion through 2030
- CAGR through 2035: 12-15%
- Strategic importance: Central to group's long-term positioning

## 4.4 Thermal Power Business

### Business Model and Strategic Role

Adani Power operates coal-fired and gas-fired thermal power plants, primarily on long-term PPAs with state electricity utilities and large industrial users.

**Existing Portfolio:**
- Udupi (1,200 MW, Karnataka)
- Tiroda (1,600 MW, Maharashtra)
- Other legacy plants and partnerships

### Financial Characteristics

- **Revenue Scale:** $3-4 billion annually
- **EBITDA Margin:** 30-35% (lower than renewables, higher than utilities)
- **Capex Requirements:** $500-800 million (maintenance and limited expansion)
- **PPA Volatility:** Subject to fuel cost pass-throughs and regulatory adjustments

### Strategic Role and Challenges

**Historical Role:** Provided baseload power generation and portfolio growth during 2010-2018.

**Current Role:** Transition from growth to stabilization as:
- Renewable competition intensifies
- Asset stranding risk increases (coal power)
- Government renewable push accelerates
- ESG pressure limits coal development

**Business Challenges:**
- Coal cost inflation
- Renewable competition pressuring PPA prices
- Long-term asset stranding risk
- Environmental regulation tightening

### Transition Strategy

Rather than aggressive expansion, thermal power strategy focuses on:
- Optimizing existing plant performance
- Extracting maximum value from existing PPAs
- Potentially monetizing or restructuring assets
- Gradual transition capacity toward renewables

## 4.5 Gas Distribution Business (Adani Gas, Adani Total Gas)

### Business Model

Adani operates city gas distribution (CGD) networks across multiple Indian cities through concession agreements with government bodies. The business distributes natural gas to CNG (transportation) and domestic/industrial users.

**Core Economics:**
- **Revenue Drivers:** Volume distributed × tariff per unit
- **Cost Structure:** Gas sourcing (~50-60% of revenue), distribution (~10-15%), overheads
- **Regulatory Framework:** Fixed return on capital (weighted average cost of capital basis)
- **Concession Duration:** 20-25 years

### Operational Scale (2025)

- **Network Coverage:** 20+ states, 100+ cities and towns
- **Customer Base:** 1 million+ households, 50,000+ commercial users
- **Capacity:** 100+ MMSCMD (million standard cubic meters per day)
- **Market Position:** ~30-35% of India's CGD market

### Financial Characteristics

- **Revenue Scale:** $2-2.5 billion annually
- **EBITDA Margin:** 45-50% (high given regulated returns)
- **Capex Requirements:** $400-600 million annually
- **Return Profile:** Regulated (8-10% WACC basis), predictable

### Strategic Importance

**Growth Drivers:**
- CNG vehicle adoption (lower cost vs. petrol/diesel)
- Industrial gas demand growth
- City expansion creating new concession opportunities
- Green gas (biogas) potential

**Advantages:**
- Regulated returns provide predictability
- Long concession terms provide visibility
- Natural monopoly characteristics protect positions
- Growing end-market demand

**Risks:**
- Vehicle electrification reducing long-term CNG demand
- Pricing regulation limiting upside
- Gas availability and sourcing risk
- Execution risk in network expansion

### Growth Trajectory

- Capacity expansion: 50-100 MMSCMD through 2035
- Geographic expansion: 50+ additional cities
- CAGR through 2035: 8-10%
- Capital deployment: $5-8 billion through 2035

## 4.6 Electricity Distribution Business

### Business Model

Adani is developing electricity distribution businesses through multiple state concessions where the business purchases power from generation sources and retails to consumers.

**Regulatory Framework:**
- Power Purchase cost pass-through
- Regulated margin (fixed return model)
- Tariff regulation by state regulators
- Multi-year regulatory cycles

### Current Scale (2025)

- **Development Stage:** Early-to-mid development
- **Pilot programs:** Multiple states
- **Future potential:** 20+ million consumer base targeting

### Strategic Rationale

- Completes energy value chain (generation → distribution)
- Captures consumer-facing relationship and data
- Positions for smart grid and digital utility evolution
- Creates synergies with renewable generation and gas distribution

### Execution Challenges

- Operational complexity and regulatory engagement required
- High fixed costs and distribution losses
- Consumer payment collection challenges (historically difficult in India)
- Capital requirements significant (₹1-2 crore per 1,000 consumers)

### Growth Trajectory

- Selective market entry in favorable states
- Gradual scale-up through organic development
- Potential partnership with established utilities
- CAGR: 15-20% as platform scales

## 4.7 Airports Business

### Business Model

Adani operates six commercial airports through 50-year government concessions, generating revenues through aeronautical charges (passenger and aircraft fees) and non-aeronautical services (retail, parking, advertising).

**Revenue Drivers:**
- Passenger volume
- Non-aeronautical retail and services
- Aircraft handling fees
- Terminal development charges

### Operational Scale (2025)

- **Airports:** 6 major facilities across India
- **Annual Passengers:** 100+ million (combined)
- **Terminal Capacity:** Under expansion to support growth
- **Non-aero Revenue:** Growing share as retail and services expand

### Financial Characteristics

- **Revenue Scale:** $800-1,200 million annually
- **EBITDA Margin:** 30-40% (higher with scale)
- **Capex Requirements:** $500-800 million annually (terminal development)
- **Growth Trajectory:** Passenger volume +7-8% CAGR

### Strategic Position

**Advantages:**
- Government concessions providing revenue security
- Growing aviation demand in India
- Minimal direct competition (airports concentrated)
- Synergies with other infrastructure assets

**Challenges:**
- Regulatory pricing controls on aeronautical charges
- Cyclical passenger traffic
- High capex for terminal development
- Geopolitical and health-related disruption risks

## 4.8 Consumer/Wilmar Business

### Business Model

Adani Wilmar operates in food processing, cooking oils, and agricultural commodities through a diversified portfolio serving both retail and institutional customers.

**Business Segments:**
- **Cooking Oils:** Consumer retail brand + institutional supplies
- **Food Grains:** Agricultural commodities trading and processing
- **Other Food Products:** Spices, flours, and specialty foods

### Operational Scale (2025)

- **Revenue Scale:** $4-5 billion annually (largest by revenue, though lower margin)
- **EBITDA Margin:** 3-5% (significantly lower than infrastructure businesses)
- **Commodity Exposure:** High sensitivity to input prices
- **Market Position:** Among India's leading food processing companies

### Strategic Assessment

**Rationale for Inclusion:**
- Diversification into consumer sector
- Leverage of supply chain capabilities
- Participation in growing food consumption

**Challenges:**
- Capital-light business conflicting with group's infrastructure focus
- Commodity price volatility and margin compression
- Different operational model and culture vs. infrastructure
- Lower strategic importance than infrastructure assets

**Portfolio Contribution:**
- Revenue contributor (large revenue base)
- EBITDA dilutive (low margins)
- Cash flow generation moderate relative to size
- Strategic role questioned by investors

## CHAPTER TAKEAWAYS

**Diversified Portfolio:** Seven major business segments reduce single-sector dependency while maintaining exposure to secular growth drivers.

**EBITDA Concentration:** Despite revenue diversification, EBITDA concentrated in three segments (Ports, Thermal, Renewable), reflecting margin differences across businesses.

**Growth Drivers:** Renewable energy and emerging utilities (gas, electricity distribution) represent future growth engines despite lower current EBITDA contribution.

**Transition Strategic:** Portfolio reflects transition from mature infrastructure (ports, thermal) toward growth-oriented renewable energy and emerging utilities.

**Portfolio Optimization:** Wilmar business (low-margin consumer) may warrant strategic review; resources potentially better deployed in higher-return infrastructure businesses.
